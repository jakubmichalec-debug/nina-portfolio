Project · FabLab, team build

A pinball machine, built from scratch.

This is a working arcade pinball machine we built in KdG's FabLab, made with two teammates. Jakub Michalec handled the mechanical build and Grigor Grigorov did the electronics. My part was all the creative work, the playfield layout, the whole visual concept, and painting every surface by hand.

Visual designPlayfield layoutMoodboardPainted by hand
Chapter 1

The idea

None of us had built a pinball machine before. The idea was just to build one from scratch, but getting there meant a lot of research and testing. It took us about six months, figuring out how the flippers should work, how the ball should move, and what we could actually make ourselves in the FabLab. Once we had a solid plan, I took the lead on how the whole thing would look and feel, which was the part I was most excited about.

The finished machine
Chapter 2

Layout & visual design

I designed the playfield layout myself, where the targets, ramps, barriers and flippers sit, and how the ball actually moves through the machine. I tested and rebuilt it many times until it felt balanced.

For the look, I moved us away from a plain school themed idea toward something more fun. I built a psychedelic, surreal mood from a Pinterest moodboard and a five color palette, and painted every visible surface by hand.

University project · Arduino + coding

A cashless drink dispenser for my Coachella festival.

This project grew straight out of my Coachella rebrand. After designing the festival and its wristbands, I got the idea for a drink stand that works with them. You tap your NFC wristband, the machine checks your balance and pours the drink by itself.

I built it myself with an Arduino, an NFC reader, a water pump in place of a real tap, and LED and LCD feedback. It has less to do with graphic design and a lot more to do with coding, and that is why I wanted to show it here.

ArduinoCodingNFCSensorsElectronics
Chapter 1

How it works

A guest taps an NFC-tagged wristband on the reader. The Arduino checks the balance: a green LED and a short pump cycle mean the drink pours, a red LED and a buzzer mean it didn't go through. A water-level sensor watches the tank and switches the LCD to “Out of Water” before it runs dry.

System diagram
Chapter 2

Building it

The whole system lives inside a hand-decorated cardboard housing: an Arduino, a 6V water pump feeding a hose from a small reservoir, a buzzer and an NFC reader, all wired on a breadboard tucked inside.

Most of the work was the code. I started with small LED tests and built it up step by step until the reader, the pump, the water sensor and the screen all worked together.

Pump, reservoir & wiring, inside the box
Chapter 3

The result

The front panel carries the LCD, the NFC reader and a cup rest under the pour spout, on a hand-drawn pattern that matches the rest of my festival work. For the NFC tags I reused the wristbands from my Coachella rebrand, so the two projects connect.
